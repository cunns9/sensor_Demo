LESSON 01

Meal prep is a system, not a recipe.

The goal isn't to cook seven complicated meals. The goal is to reduce decisions during the week.

LESSON 03

Your weekly meal prep workflow.

Use the same workflow every week until it becomes automatic.

1

Check what you already have

Look through your refrigerator, freezer, and pantry. Build meals around food you already own first.

2

Choose 2–3 meals

Keep the menu small. Repeating meals makes prep faster and reduces food waste.

3

Create one shopping list

Organize the list by proteins, produce, carbs, pantry items, and extras.

4

Cook in parallel

Put something in the oven while another item cooks on the stove and vegetables are being prepared.

5

Portion and store

Refrigerate meals you'll eat soon and freeze meals intended for later in the week.

LESSON 04

Store food strategically.

Proper storage prevents your carefully prepared meals from turning into food waste.

R

Refrigerator

Keep the meals you'll eat first within easy reach.

Best for:
  • Next few meals
  • Fresh vegetables
  • Prepared breakfast
  • Ready-to-eat snacks
F

Freezer

Freeze meals you won't eat soon to extend their useful life.

Best for:
  • Cooked proteins
  • Rice dishes
  • Soups
  • Extra portions
S

Separate

Store components separately when textures matter.

Separate:
  • Sauces
  • Crunchy toppings
  • Salad dressing
  • Fresh garnishes

THE RULES

Keep meal prep simple.

01

Don't prep everything.

Prep the foods that remove the most work from your week.

02

Repeat ingredients.

One ingredient can become several meals with different flavors.

03

Prep for reality.

Your meal plan should fit your actual schedule, not your ideal one.

04

Improve gradually.

Every week, notice what you ate, wasted, enjoyed, and avoided.
